Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026-10-28

The short version. Unstall is a free Mac app. It runs on your Mac. Your agent sessions, their commands and prompts, and your voice audio are not sent to us. The answers you give go to your own agents, over SSH if they run on another machine you added, and if you add your own OpenRouter key, the text of a card goes to OpenRouter to write its spoken summary. If you pair Unstall for iPhone, card text goes from your Mac to your iPhone over Tailscale, and your Mac sends content-free pushes through our push relay at unstall.dev, which stores nothing. The app sends a few anonymous usage events (on by default, one switch in Settings turns them off), checks for updates, and downloads voice models only if you turn voice on. You can give us an email address or a feedback score if you want to; you never have to. We do not sell data.

What we collect, and what we never collect

What we collect

  • A random install id created on your Mac and saved in Unstall’s preferences
  • When the app starts: its version, build and macOS version
  • When an agent connects: its kind (Claude, Codex, Grok, Muse, OpenCode or pi)
  • When an answer you gave to an approval goes through: the agent kind, your decision (allow, always allow or deny), and whether you clicked or spoke
  • When you turn voice mode on or off
  • That you skipped the email ask
  • Your email address, only if you enter it and press Send
  • A 0–10 feedback score and an optional one-line reason (up to 280 characters), only if you press Send
  • On unstall.dev: a page view when you open the home page, and which Download button you click
  • From Unstall for iPhone, if its usage stats are on: the events listed under Unstall for iPhone and the push relay
  • Through the push relay, only to deliver a push and not stored: the items listed in that section

What we never collect

  • Commands, file paths or prompts
  • What your agents print or ask
  • Session, project or host names
  • Voice transcripts or audio
  • Your SSH keys, API keys or agent settings
  • Cookies on unstall.dev
  • Any card text through the push relay

That is the complete list. Routine app events carry only the install id and the values above, each from a fixed set. Unstall does not read commands, prompts, paths, session or host names, or transcripts for them.

What stays on your Mac

Unstall reads what your agents are doing so it can show their questions in the notch. It gets this from herdr (through herdr’s local socket), from the launcher and hooks you connect, and from the visible text of the terminal pane where an agent is waiting. All of that is processed on your Mac and is not sent to us. If you pair an iPhone, your Mac sends the cards it shows to that iPhone, directly over Tailscale (below).

Unstall stores its own files on your Mac: its settings in macOS preferences, records and backups for the agents you connect in ~/Library/Application Support/Unstall, status files in ~/Library/Application Support/NotchAgent, and, for pi, question files in ~/.pi/agent/notch/asks. When you choose Connect for an agent, Unstall adds marked hook entries to that agent’s settings (for example ~/.claude/settings.json or ~/.codex/hooks.json) and keeps a backup. Disconnect removes them.

Voice mode

Voice mode is off by default. When you turn it on in Settings, Unstall:

  • Downloads its speech models (Kokoro for speech, Parakeet for recognition, Silero for detecting when you stop talking) from Hugging Face (huggingface.co), at fixed versions. Hugging Face receives these download requests, including your IP address, like any download.
  • Speaks and listens on your Mac. Microphone audio is never saved to disk or sent anywhere. What you say is turned into text on your Mac and used to pick your answer; transcripts are not sent to us. If you dictate a free-form reply and confirm it, that text is sent to your agent as your answer, just as if you had typed it. For Claude sessions started with the notch-agent launcher, the answer is also written to a private file in that session’s run folder so Claude can read it.
  • Unless you add an OpenRouter key (below), writes the sentence it speaks on your Mac, with Apple’s on-device model where available or a fixed template otherwise.

Optional OpenRouter summaries. If you paste your own OpenRouter API key into Settings → Voice, Unstall sends the one command, permission title or question from the card (at most 400 characters, with obvious secrets removed) to OpenRouter to write the spoken sentence. Unstall does not add the agent or project name, but the command or question itself may contain names and file paths, and those are sent as they are. Cards that mention credentials, and commands on Unstall’s fixed risky list, are never sent. Your key is kept in the macOS Keychain. This is the only way card text goes to a third party (your own paired iPhone aside), it happens only with your key, and OpenRouter’s own terms and privacy policy apply to it. Remove the key to stop it.

Unstall for iPhone and the push relay

Unstall for iPhone shows what your agents are waiting on and lets you answer. It talks to Unstall on each Mac you pair, directly over Tailscale, with a certificate pinned at pairing. Commands, questions, answers, agent output, and project, session and Mac names travel only on that connection, and never through us.

To wake the iPhone when an agent is waiting, your Mac asks our push relay at unstall.dev/api to send a push through Apple’s push service. Every push carries the same fixed text, “Agent waiting”, and no prompt content. The iPhone then fetches the card from your Mac over Tailscale.

What the relay receives

  • When the iPhone gets push permission (after install, then about once a week): its push token, whether it is a production or development build, the app’s identifier, and App Attest data, which proves the request comes from the genuine Unstall app on a real iPhone. The relay returns a signed grant that contains the push token and expires after 30 days. The iPhone gives the grant to the Macs it is paired with.
  • For each push, from your Mac: the grant (which contains the push token), two random IDs (one for the Mac, one for the waiting item), whether to show or clear a notification, and an expiry time.

What it stores: nothing. The relay has no database and keeps nothing between requests. Its own log records only which endpoint was called and the outcome, not the token, grant or IDs. Vercel’s request log keeps the IP address, path and status of each request under Vercel’s retention.

What never reaches the relay: card text, commands, prompts, answers, agent output, names or file paths. There is no database, and pushes carry no prompt content.

On the iPhone

  • The credentials for each paired Mac and the push grant are kept in the iPhone’s Keychain. Which setup steps you ticked is kept in the app’s preferences.
  • Anonymous usage stats, on by default, sent to PostHog with a random install id: the app version, build and iOS version when it starts; whether pairing and getting push permission worked; whether you opened an item from the app or a notification; the kind of answer you gave (for example approve, deny or pick an option) and whether it went through; and whether a notification got its details and how it was cleared. Each value comes from a fixed set; no card text, names or paths. Turn off Share anonymous usage stats on the Macs tab to stop them.

On the Mac

Unstall keeps each paired iPhone’s credentials, push token and grant in the macOS Keychain. Revoke an iPhone in Settings → Connections and that Mac stops answering it and sending it pushes.

Network connections the app makes

Remote machines over SSH

Unstall connects only to hosts you add, using your own ssh setup and keys. It never asks for a password. It forwards herdr’s socket on that machine to your Mac to read agent state. The connection runs directly between your Mac and your machine and does not pass through us.

Your iPhone and the push relay

Only after you pair an iPhone: Unstall accepts connections from that iPhone over Tailscale, and sends push requests to the relay at unstall.dev/api over HTTPS, as described above. The relay and Vercel receive your Mac’s IP address with each request.

Updates

Unstall uses Sparkle to check for updates. It checks automatically by default; you can turn automatic checks off in Settings or use Check for Updates in the menu instead. Checks read our update feed on GitHub Pages (jonkthomas.github.io/unstall-releases), and updates download from GitHub releases. GitHub receives these requests, including your IP address and the app version, like any web request. Unstall does not send a system profile with update checks. Updates are signed, and Unstall checks the signature before installing. By default it does not install updates on its own: you choose Install & Relaunch. Sparkle’s update window also offers to install future updates automatically, and that happens only if you turn it on there.

Usage analytics

The app events in the list above are sent to PostHog (US cloud), which processes them for us. They are tied to the random install id, not to your name, Apple ID or hardware. Usage stats are on by default, and Unstall shows a notice about them in its panel. Turning off Share anonymous usage stats in Settings stops routine sending immediately and cancels any request still waiting to go; events already sent cannot be recalled. PostHog receives your IP address as part of each request, as any web service does. Unstall tells PostHog not to look up a location from it, so events are not tagged with a location.

Email and feedback, if you choose

  • Email. On the third day you use Unstall, or from Settings → Get updates by email, Unstall may ask for your email address so we can send you news about Unstall. If you enter it and press Send, it is stored in PostHog with your install id. That links the email to this install’s usage events and feedback, including those sent before, so they are no longer anonymous. If you skip while usage stats are on, the only thing sent is that you skipped.
  • Feedback score. On the seventh day you use Unstall, or from Settings → Give feedback, Unstall may ask how likely you are to recommend it (0–10), with an optional one-line reason of up to 280 characters. They are sent to PostHog with your install id only when you press Send. The ask comes back at most once every 90 days. Please do not put confidential information in the reason.
  • Only one ask is shown at a time, never while an agent is waiting for you, and each can be skipped. With usage stats off, the asks are still offered. Pressing Send is your consent, so an email or feedback answer you send goes to PostHog even while usage stats are off; nothing else does.

unstall.dev

The site uses PostHog with nothing stored in your browser: no cookies and no local storage, so each visit counts as a new visitor. It records a page view when you open the home page, and which Download button you click (header, hero or bottom). Each carries the page address without its query, the referring site (its origin only, never the full address) and any campaign (UTM) tags in the link. There is no session replay and no automatic capture of clicks. PostHog receives your IP address as part of each request; the site tells it not to look up a location. We never connect site visits to app installs. The site is hosted by Vercel, which processes requests, including your IP address, to serve it. The Download button takes you to GitHub, where GitHub’s policies apply.

How we use information

  • To see which versions are in use, which agents people connect, and whether approvals and voice work, so we can fix and improve Unstall.
  • To send news about Unstall to an email address you gave us. Every email will let you stop receiving them.
  • To read feedback and decide what to build next.

Who we share it with

We do not sell personal information and do not share it for advertising. Service providers process it for us:

  • PostHog: app, iPhone app and site analytics, email and feedback answers (United States).
  • Vercel: hosting for unstall.dev and the push relay.
  • Apple: push delivery to your iPhone (the push token and the fixed push), and App Attest.
  • GitHub: the update feed and app downloads.
  • Hugging Face: voice model downloads, only if you turn voice on.
  • OpenRouter: only if you add your own key, under your own account with them.

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, to protect people’s safety or our rights, or as part of a merger, acquisition or sale of assets, in which case this policy continues to apply to it.

Where it is processed

PostHog stores our analytics in its US cloud. The push relay runs on Vercel in its iad1 region (Washington, D.C., United States). Apple processes push delivery and App Attest. If you are outside the United States, the information above is transferred to and processed there.

How long we keep it

We keep usage events only as long as they help us improve Unstall. We keep an email address until you ask us to delete it or stop receiving emails, and delete it on request. The push relay keeps nothing between requests; Vercel keeps its request logs under its own retention. A push grant expires 30 days after it is issued.

Your choices and rights

  • Turn off Share anonymous usage stats in Settings at any time.
  • Skip the email and feedback asks, or never open them.
  • Leave voice mode off, or remove your OpenRouter key.
  • Turn automatic update checks off in Settings.
  • Turn off Share anonymous usage stats on the iPhone app’s Macs tab. Revoke a paired iPhone in the Mac’s Settings → Connections, or forget a Mac on the iPhone.
  • To remove Unstall completely: remove your OpenRouter key in Settings → Voice if you added one (it is kept in your Keychain), disconnect your agents in Settings, quit and delete the app, then delete ~/Library/Application Support/Unstall, ~/Library/Application Support/NotchAgent and, if you installed the screen saver, ~/Library/Screen Savers/NotchAgent.saver. Its settings, including the remote hosts you added, stay in macOS preferences until you run defaults delete co.schoolintel.notch-pi in Terminal.

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal information we hold, or to object to how we use it. To ask, email hello@unstall.dev. If you gave us an email address, write from it so we can find your record. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Security

The app’s connections to PostHog, GitHub, Hugging Face, OpenRouter and the push relay use HTTPS. The iPhone talks to your Mac over Tailscale with a certificate pinned at pairing. Updates are signed and checked before they install. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.
